# Department of Intergalactic Vibrations

**D.I.V.** — *Maintaining the Resonance Grid since 12,847 BCE.*

A fictional cosmic agency we play as live characters at festivals (starting with Seven Stars). We're field crew posted to Earth. Our job: keep the galactic Resonance Grid running, and investigate vibrational anomalies. Earth's been generating a lot of anomalies lately. Mostly festivals. We're not complaining.

---

## The Pitch (one breath)

> "We're with the Department of Intergalactic Vibrations — D.I.V. We maintain the Resonance Grid. Your sector's been throwing some really beautiful readings lately, so we've been getting assigned here a lot. Mind if I take a quick measurement? It'll only take a second."

The bit works because **we're a utility crew doing serious work on something joyful.** We treat vibes with the full technical seriousness of an electrician inspecting a junction box. The joke is the contrast.

---

## The Universe

**D.I.V.** is an old, sprawling galactic agency that maintains the Resonance Grid — the energy infrastructure that runs through the galaxy. Nodes, junctions, conduits. We inspect connections, patch leaks, log anomalies. There are crews on most inhabited planets.

**Earth's grid presence** is small but increasingly active. Festivals, in particular, generate massive vibrational spikes that read as anomalies to HQ — so crews keep getting dispatched. Officially we're investigating. Unofficially: we love it here. The vibes on Earth are genuinely great and we're not in a hurry to leave.

**No conspiracy.** We're not hiding from HQ. We just genuinely enjoy Earth assignments and keep volunteering. Good food, good music, weather's nice. You people are doing something special and we're allowed to appreciate it.

### How the work is organized: the stars

The Grid isn't laid out on arbitrary coordinates — it's organized by **constellations and stellar alignments.** Earth's astrology charts are, by cosmic coincidence, a surprisingly accurate map of how the agency divvies up its work. Field offices are named by constellation. Sectors are mapped to houses. Shift schedules track planetary transits.

We don't *believe in* astrology — we *use* it, because it's literally how the Grid is organized. When we tell someone their Mercury reading is elevated, we mean it operationally. Different posture than a tarot reader: we're technicians, and the stars are our paperwork.

### Seven Stars = Pleiades

The Seven Stars festival is named after the **Pleiades** — a real star cluster, and an active node on our Grid. The crew working Seven Stars is the **Pleiades Field Office** detachment. The festival sits on a Pleiades-aligned anomaly: the readings here pulse in a specific signature that traces back to that cluster.

This lets us tie crew identity, patches, and signage to the festival specifically (Pleiades sector insignia) without locking the broader brand to *only* Seven Stars forever. Different festival next year, different field office.

## Tone & Voice

**Whimsical, goofy, good-vibes — with a touch of MIB mystery.** We're the kind of people you actually want to talk to at a festival. We're playful, curious, and genuinely interested in Earth. The mystery is flavor — we never quite confirm we're aliens, but we don't deny it either.

### Three flavors we blend, depending on the moment:
- **Earth-fan tourists** — wide-eyed wonder at mundane things. "Wait, *this* is pizza? Incredible."
- **Vibes-as-serious-science** — full technical jargon, professional measurements, frowning at gadget readings. Deeply credentialed about something silly.
- **Playful dodging** — never quite confirm where we're from. Change the subject. Ask them questions instead. They leave unsure what just happened, and smiling.

### Voice rules
- **Treat resonance as a real technical discipline.** Use grid jargon. Take readings seriously.
- **Never punch down.** The joke is never on the person in front of us.
- **Don't claim outright to be aliens.** Imply. Dodge. Smile.
- **Be the thing.** We're literally good-vibes workers. The character is *not* cynical, jaded, or sarcastic.
- **Off-duty is real.** Out of uniform at camp, we're just people.

### Sample phrases
- "Routine grid inspection. Just passing through."
- "Your sector's reading beautifully today. Have you had your levels checked?"
- "I'm going to need to log this. Strictly procedural."
- "Above my pay grade, friend. But the readings are *great.*"
- "We *can* talk about where we're from. We just won't."
- "This is one of our favorite postings, honestly."

## Visual Identity

**Costume base:** purple hi-vis vest, purple hard hat, ID badge on lanyard, hand-held gadget. Real utility-crew gear, recolored. The construction-worker silhouette is the signature.

**Palette:** deep cosmic purple (primary), holographic/iridescent silver (secondary), reflective hi-vis striping. Black for type.

**Graphic aesthetic:** *cosmic industrial.* Trade union / OSHA poster bones (bold, blocky, stencil-y) crossed with retro space program flourishes (mission patches, roundels, vintage scientific labels). Imagine a utility company that operates in space. Every printed artifact lives in this world.

**Texture layer:** psychedelic. Holographic foils, iridescent surfaces, gradients bleeding purple → magenta → cyan, swirling/melting patterns. These ride *on top* of the rigid industrial bones — OSHA-poster structure with lava-lamp skin. The contrast is the joke and the look.

**Iconography:** star charts, constellation lines, planetary glyphs, astrological house diagrams — drawn as if they were electrical schematics. Star maps as wiring diagrams. The Pleiades cluster as a recurring motif.

**Anti-aesthetic:** not New Age. Not crystals-and-mandalas. Not sci-fi-movie chrome. Not modern startup. We're a *trade.*

## Interaction Tiers

We engage at three depths. We adjust on the fly based on how much someone leans in.

- **Tier 1 — Slap & go.** Quick reading, slap an inspection sticker on them, a brief remark, move on. High volume. Most people get this.
- **Tier 2 — Commendation.** For notable vibes or longer chats. Hand out a numbered card recognizing something specific. They feel seen.
- **Tier 3 — Recruit.** For the ones who really lean in. The full bit — induct them as a provisional field agent, pin them with a badge, deeper conversation. They become part of the universe for the weekend.

The artifacts (stickers, cards, badges, gadgets, totem) get their own doc. This doc is just the *why.*

## On-Duty / Off-Duty Rule

**In uniform = in character.** Vest on, hat on, gadget out — we're agents. We don't break.

**At camp without uniform = off-duty.** Civilian clothes, civilian behavior. People who recognize us get a knowing nod. *"Off the clock, friend."*

This rule protects us from burnout and keeps the bit sharp.

## What D.I.V. Is NOT

- Not spiritual / New Age / earnestly mystical — we're absurdist about vibes.
- Not cynical or jaded — we genuinely love this job and this planet.
- Not a club anyone can join by asking — recruitment is at the crew's discretion. That's what makes the badge feel earned.
- Not topical or political.
- Not high-tech-futuristic — we're retro-utility-cosmic. Think NASA 1968 meets electrician's union, not SpaceX 2025.
