# Transmissions

Every field agent's walkie-talkie is theoretically connected to [[hq]]. Most of the time, the channel is open and nothing's on it. Sometimes HQ answers. Sometimes something else does.

## What comes through

Field reports collected across decades describe a consistent inventory of non-HQ transmissions:

- **Fragments of old broadcasts.** Radio plays from the 1940s. News bulletins from places that no longer exist. Music from before recording was invented (which shouldn't be possible).
- **Voices.** Often calm. Often in a language nobody on shift recognizes. Occasionally in a language the agent recognizes from childhood but hasn't spoken in years.
- **A single note held for a long time.** Hum, drone, sustained tone. Agents report feeling it more than hearing it.
- **A song you almost recognize.** You can't quite place it. You think about it for weeks.
- **Wind.** Just wind. But the kind of wind you can tell is somewhere specific. A room. A canyon. A long hallway.

## What it means

We don't know. The official position is that the walkie picks up Grid bleed-through — local resonance translated into something the human ear can parse. Field agents have less official theories. Some think it's other field offices, faintly. Some think it's [[seven]]. Some think it's the agency itself, talking to itself across time.

See [[empty-hq]] for one of the stranger possibilities.

## Field guidance

Use the walkie anyway. Even when the channel is just wind, use it. Reports across the agency consistently show that walkie use correlates with stronger local readings, regardless of whether anyone responds. The act of calling out is part of the work.
