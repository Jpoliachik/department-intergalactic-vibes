# Field Doctrine

The principles that guide the Field Service. Not strictly written down anywhere official, which is part of the point. Passed agent to agent.

## The doctrine, in five lines

1. **Show up.** In person. In uniform. On time-ish.
2. **Witness the signal.** Take readings. Listen. Notice what's working.
3. **Add to it.** Your presence is part of the reading. You're not outside the Grid taking measurements; you're inside the Grid being measured. Dance.
4. **Patch quietly.** Where connection has gone thin, gently feed it. A conversation. A commendation. A sticker. Don't make a production of the patch.
5. **Don't manage the Grid.** The Grid does not need to be managed. The Grid needs to be tended, like a fire — fed, not controlled.

## Why this matters

Most of [[the-department]]'s history is paperwork-side: remote readings, archived reports, occasional protocols. Field Service is the wing that says the work has to happen in person, on the ground, with the beings making the signal. Some at [[hq]] still consider this a soft science. Field agents consider HQ a soft science.

There is no formal antagonism between Field and HQ. There is a difference in posture. Some agents privately suspect Field Doctrine is a course correction the agency made — quietly, slowly, over generations — after centuries of doing the work too coldly. We're not sure when that correction started. We're not sure it's finished.

## Tier system

How field agents engage on shift:

- **Tier 1 — Reading.** Quick measurement, brief observation, inspection sticker, move on. Most of a shift.
- **Tier 2 — Commendation.** Numbered card for an unusually clean reading or exemplary conduct.
- **Tier 3 — Provisional induction.** Bring someone in as a Provisional Field Agent for the duration of the event.

Escalation is by agent gut. Don't escalate on request — escalation is based on what *you* observe. Your gut counts as a reading. Trust it.

## Posture

Bureaucratic-but-warm. Technical-but-mystical. Take the work seriously. Take yourself lightly. The Grid likes laughter.
